CATEGORY: Academic Integrity

TITLE: University of Mauritius - Plagiarism, Fabrication and Academic Integrity

INFORMATION:

Plagiarism and fabrication or falsification of results/documents are treated as serious academic offences by the University of Mauritius.

1. PLAGIARISM

Plagiarism involves using another person's work and presenting it as one's own, whether the work is published or unpublished.

Examples include:

* Submitting another person's work as one's own
* Directly reproducing material without proper citation
* Paraphrasing or summarising another person's work without acknowledging the source
* Using facts, figures, graphs, charts or information without acknowledging the source
* Downloading material from the Internet and presenting it as one's own without acknowledgement
* Copyright infringement

2. ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

Students are expected to submit their own work.

Students should clearly identify and acknowledge sources used in their assignments, projects, dissertations and other assessed work.

Students should acknowledge the ideas, concepts, experiments and results of other people even when those ideas are expressed in their own words.

3. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

The University regulations emphasise responsible and ethical use of Artificial Intelligence.

The use of AI must be appropriately attributed and cited where required.

Students must not misuse AI in a way that compromises academic integrity, authorship or the credibility of their work.

4. FABRICATION AND FALSIFICATION

Fabrication or falsification of results/documents is treated as an academic offence.

Students must not fabricate or falsify academic results, data or documents.

5. PROJECTS AND DISSERTATIONS

Students submitting projects or dissertations are required to comply with the University's academic integrity requirements.

The University uses Turnitin for relevant project/dissertation submissions.

6. CONSEQUENCES

Plagiarism is considered a serious offence and may result in disciplinary action.

Depending on the circumstances and applicable University procedures, penalties may include reduction of academic classification or expulsion.

The University also states that a qualification may be forfeited if plagiarism is detected after an award has been made.
